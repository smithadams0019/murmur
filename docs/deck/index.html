The knowledge is already in the room

Nothing in that gap is a knowledge problem.

What is missing is the echo. So Murmur does not diagnose rheumatic heart disease, or anything else. It listens to a heart-sound recording and says whether a murmur is audible enough to be worth an echocardiogram.

The decision to refer belongs to a clinician. The tool only decides who is put in front of one.

What is finished, and what is not claimed

The model is trained, evaluated and deployed. That is the whole of it.

It diagnoses nothing

A murmur is not rheumatic heart disease. The output is a prompt to arrange an echocardiogram, with the segment that drove it available to listen to.

No outcome claim

Murmur has never been deployed in a clinic or trialled. The Marijon trial establishes that screening finds cases examination misses. It says nothing about this software.

One corpus, one set of equipment

CirCor was recorded in a single screening programme with a digital stethoscope. Behaviour on a phone microphone, in another population, in a noisy clinic, is unmeasured.

Nothing leaves the device

A 575 KB model runs client-side in the browser. No recording is uploaded, stored or transmitted. Not a medical device.

The knowledge is already in the room. What fails is access to the test. Murmur knows nothing a nurse does not. It decides who reaches the machine.
